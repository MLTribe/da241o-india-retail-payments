The classification rule

Absolute contraction, relative share loss or continued growth

Drawdown D = latest 12-month average volume ÷ the method's own highest 12-month average − 1. Share change = latest 12-month average share − reference-window average, within the primary group. Absolute contraction if D ≤ −10%; otherwise relative share loss if the share fell; otherwise continued growth. A label is robust if at least four of five variants agree, including the PSI-period window.

Why shares fell

Own growth against comparison-group growth

Log share change = log own growth − log group growth, between the reference window and the latest 12 months. A share can fall because the method shrank or because the group (mostly UPI, much of it payments that were never digital) grew faster.

Hypotheses

H1–H3: the Assignment 2 rule and the sharper test

H1 · Small payments move to UPI, so IMPS and debit tickets rise

A2 rule: the OLS trend (month, Diwali and COVID dummies, Newey-West 12) in log relative ticket and in log real ticket must both be positive at 5% (one-sided). v2: the same on the mean 12-month log change, with the relative ticket split into own ticket and UPI's own falling ticket.

H2 · The shifts coincide with COVID, not with zero MDR

Least-squares search for a level-and-trend break (15% trimming) with month, Diwali and COVID-month dummies for both waves, so the lockdown collapse itself cannot be the break. 95% confidence sets invert the likelihood-ratio statistic (Hansen 2000). BIC chooses 0, 1 or 2 breaks. Indeterminate when the set covers both Jan 2020 and Mar 2020.

Card bridge: bank-wise statistics against RBI PSI

Before Jun 2021 the card series are RBI bank-wise totals multiplied by k; from Jun 2021 they are RBI PSI. The out-of-sample check compares k × bank-wise with PSI "same month last year" figures for Jun 2020 – May 2021.

Diagnostics

Unit roots, trend tests and residual autocorrelation

ADF with constant, trend, month dummies and 2 lags (5% critical value −3.45); KPSS for trend stationarity with 12 Bartlett lags (5% critical value 0.146). Most log levels look persistent, which is why the primary trend tests use mean 12-month log changes rather than OLS trend t-statistics.
