2 · The card bridge (splice)

Joining two RBI card sources into one series

See the bridge table on the results page →

aThe question

RBI publishes card payments in two places that do not line up before mid-2021. How do we build one consistent monthly series of debit- and credit-card payments (volume and value) from , and how do we know the join is sound?

bThe two sources and why they differ

  • RBI Payment System Indicators (PSI) is RBI's monthly release of payment statistics for every payment system (RTGS, NEFT, UPI, IMPS, cards, prepaid instruments and so on). Its card tables (4.1 debit, 4.2 credit) split card payments into "PoS based" and "Others" (mostly online); the total of the two is every card payment. RBI reclassified card data in Jun 2021, and the PSI card series is consistently classified only from .
  • RBI bank-wise ATM/PoS/card statistics are monthly pages with one row per bank (cards issued, ATMs, PoS terminals, card use) and an all-bank "Total" row, available back to 2014. In the layout used before Mar 2022 the page reports card use only "at ATM" and "at PoS". That "at PoS" column in fact covers every card payment, online included: in the overlap months it equals PSI "PoS based" + "Others", not PSI "PoS based" alone. So the bank-wise PoS column maps to the PSI total.
  • From Mar 2022 the bank-wise pages use a new layout with separate PoS, online and other columns per bank. The all-bank totals file (rbi/cards_bankwise_2014_2022.csv) therefore ends in Feb 2022.

The five bridging assumptions (from analysis/long_series.py)

    cFormulas

    k = \exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{9}\sum_{m=\text{Jun 2021}}^{\text{Feb 2022}} \ln \frac{\text{PSI}_m}{B_m}\Big) \qquad \text{(geometric mean of the nine overlap ratios)}
    X_t = \begin{cases} \text{PSI}_t & t \ge \text{Jun 2021} \\ k \times B_t & t \lt \text{Jun 2021} \end{cases}

    B is the bank-wise all-bank total (PoS column) and PSI the PSI total (PoS based + Others), in the same unit. k is worked out four times: debit volume, debit value, credit volume, credit value. Card stocks (cards outstanding, PoS terminals) and ATM withdrawals are not scaled.

    e_m = \Big|\frac{k \times B_m}{\text{PSI}^{\text{prior}}_m} - 1\Big|, \quad \text{MAPE} = 100 \times \frac{1}{12}\sum_m e_m, \quad \text{max error} = 100 \times \max_m e_m

    The out-of-sample check: each PSI release from Jun 2021 to May 2022 also prints the same month one year earlier ("same month last year"). Those columns give PSI-consistent figures for , months that were not used to estimate k. If the bridged series k × B is close to them, the bridge works outside the months that fixed it.

    dDecision rule and checks

    The splice is accepted if (1) the nine overlap ratios are close to one another, so a single k describes them; (2) k × B is close to the PSI "same month last year" figures out of sample; and (3) the average ticket does not jump at the bank-wise unit change in Jul 2019 (the pages switched from ₹ million to ₹ lakh). In the regressions a level dummy from absorbs any step left at the splice (assumption 5).

    The overlap table on the results page

    The "Card bridge" table on the results page compares the bank-wise figures with PSI month by month, – ( months). Up to Feb 2022 the bank-wise number is the all-bank total (PoS column); from Mar 2022 it is the sum over banks of PoS + online + other from the new-layout pages. The gap is bank-wise ÷ PSI − 1.

    eWorked example (live numbers)

    fCaveats

    • From Mar 2022 the bank-level sums match PSI to within 0.01% in only of months per series (largest gap ). They are not identical, and since the panel uses PSI itself from Jun 2021, this comparison mainly confirms the transcription of the bank-level pages rather than testing the definitions.
    • The Jun 2021 level dummy is a weak test of assumption 5 because Jun 2021 is also the last month of COVID's second wave: a recovery step and a bridge error look the same.
    • The bridged series before Jun 2021 can only be as good as the bank-wise definitions; k corrects levels, not month-to-month reporting differences.

    3 · The answer

    How the headline paragraph and the verdict cards are built

    See the answer →

    aThe question

    Did IMPS, debit cards and credit cards contract in absolute terms, only lose share, or keep growing as UPI expanded; and do hypotheses H1–H3 hold?

    bData used

    No new calculation: the headline is assembled from the primary classification (section 7) and the hypothesis verdicts (section 9).

    c–dHow the text and labels are produced

    • Headline sentence 1 (debit): the debit drawdown D at the 10% primary threshold and the month its 12-month average peaked.
    • Sentence 2 (IMPS): IMPS's label, its drawdown and peak month. It is called "the boundary case" because its label changes with the threshold.
    • Sentence 3 (credit): credit's label and its share change within P2M + debit + credit.
    • Verdict cards: each hypothesis has two labels, shown side by side and never merged into one. The left label applies the rejection rule stated in Assignment 2 ("Not rejected" / "Rejected"). The right label is the sharper "v2" test added after the review, which can come out "Supported", "Partially supported", "Not supported" or "Indeterminate". The exact rules are in section 9.

    6 · Pre vs post COVID

    Growth and share by period

    See the table →

    aThe question

    How fast did each rail grow before, during and after COVID, did its share in its group rise or fall in each period, and did growth change between the pre- and post-COVID periods?

    bData used

    Volumes (upi_volume_mn, imps_volume_mn, p2m_volume_mn, debit_volume_mn, credit_volume_mn) in logs; shares within UPI + IMPS for UPI and IMPS, and within P2M + debit + credit for P2M and cards. Periods:

    cFormulas

    g_t = \ln N_t - \ln N_{t-12} \qquad \text{(the change on the same month a year earlier, in log points)}

    A month t counts if it lies in the period, its base month t − 12 is not earlier than the period's base start, both months are observed, and (UPI only) the pair does not straddle the Aug 2018 same-account exclusion (t − 12 < Aug 2018 ≤ t). At least 3 comparisons are needed.

    \hat g = \frac{1}{n}\sum_t g_t, \qquad \text{SE}(\hat g)^2 = \frac{1}{n^2}\Big[\sum_t e_t^2 + 2\sum_{l=1}^{12}\Big(1-\frac{l}{13}\Big)\sum_{t} e_t e_{t-l}\Big],\quad e_t = g_t - \hat g

    This is the Newey–West (HAC) standard error with Bartlett weights and 12 lags, from an OLS regression of g on a constant. It allows for the strong overlap between neighbouring 12-month changes.

    \text{growth a year} = 100\,(e^{\hat g}-1)\%, \qquad 95\%\ \text{CI} = 100\,(e^{\hat g \pm 1.96\,\text{SE}}-1)\%
    \Delta = \hat g_{\text{post}} - \hat g_{\text{pre}}, \qquad \text{SE}(\Delta) = \sqrt{\text{SE}_{\text{pre}}^2 + \text{SE}_{\text{post}}^2}

    Shares: sfrom is the mean share in the first three months of the period that have share data, sto the mean of the last three.

    dDecision rule

    Base-start rule. Pre-COVID and post-COVID compare months only with bases inside the same period. The COVID period is the exception: its base start is moved 12 months earlier, so COVID months are compared with the same month before COVID, on purpose.
    Class rule for each cell, in order: Absolute contraction if the upper end of the 95% CI is below zero (ĝ + 1.96 SE < 0); otherwise Relative share loss if the share fell (sto < sfrom); otherwise Co-expansion. The post − pre column is coloured red if its whole 95% interval is below zero and green if it is above.

    eWorked example (live numbers)

    fCaveats

    • Debit and credit have no P2M + cards share before Apr 2020, so in the pre-COVID period they can only be "Absolute contraction" or "Co-expansion".
    • n counts overlapping 12-month comparisons, not independent observations; that is why the HAC correction matters.
    • The difference's SE treats the two periods as independent, which ignores any correlation across the boundary.

    7 · The classification rule

    Absolute contraction, relative share loss or continued growth

    See the classification →

    aThe question

    For IMPS, debit cards and credit cards: has the rail actually shrunk (fewer payments than at its best), has it only lost share to UPI while still growing, or has it kept growing in both senses?

    bData used

    Primary variant: monthly volume (imps_volume_mn, debit_volume_mn, credit_volume_mn), – ; shares within the primary group (share_imps_ui_pct for IMPS; share_debit_pc_pct, share_credit_pc_pct for cards). Reference windows for the share change:

    cFormulas

    A_t = \frac{1}{12}\sum_{j=0}^{11} X_{t-j} \qquad \text{(12-month average, only if all 12 months are in the window)}
    P = \arg\max_t A_t, \qquad D = \frac{A_T}{A_P} - 1 \qquad (T = \text{last month of the window})
    \Delta s = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{12}\textstyle\sum_{t=T-11}^{T} s_t}_{\text{latest 12 months}} - \underbrace{\overline{s}_{\text{reference window}}}_{\text{mean monthly share}} \quad \text{(percentage points)}

    D is never positive: if the latest 12-month average is the highest so far, P = T and D = 0. The share terms are averages of monthly shares.

    dDecision rule

    For threshold θ (; primary ):
    1. Absolute contraction if D ≤ −θ;
    2. otherwise Relative share loss if Δs < 0;
    3. otherwise Continued growth.
    Robustness. The primary label (volume, 10%, primary group, full window) is compared with five checks: threshold 5%, threshold 20%, real value, PSI-period window, and either RBI daily data (IMPS) or the secondary group (cards). The label is robust if at least 4 of the 5 agree (n − 1 of n) and the PSI-period window agrees; otherwise it is "fragile".

    The variants

    • Real value: X is CPI-deflated value (*_real_value_crore) and the share is a share of nominal value within the group (CPI cancels out of a value share).
    • PSI-period window: the window starts in , so the first 12-month average ends in May 2022 and the reference window is . This uses only consistently classified RBI card data.
    • RBI daily data (IMPS only): UPI and IMPS volumes summed from RBI's daily file, window , reference .
    • Secondary group (cards): share within UPI total + debit + credit, reference .
    • Within cards (cards, shown in the table only, not one of the five checks): share within debit + credit, reference .

    Crossing month, heat-map and Fig 4

    d_t = \frac{A_t}{\max_{u \le t} A_u} - 1 \qquad \text{(drawdown from the running peak)}

    Crossing month for threshold θ: the first month of the spell with dt ≤ −θ that is still running at the end of the window. If dt climbs back above −θ, the clock resets; if the latest month is above −θ, there is no crossing. Fig 4 plots dt × 100 for the three primary variants, with reference lines at −5%, −10% and −20%. The heat-map applies the same rule at 5%, 10% and 20% to three variants: "Volume, bridged" (primary), "Real value" and "Volume, PSI period".

    eWorked example (live numbers)

    Every variant, with D, Δs and the labels at 5 / 10 / 20%

    fCaveats

    • The thresholds are conventions. A rail between two thresholds (IMPS now) gets different labels at 10% and 20%, which is why IMPS is reported as the boundary case.
    • IMPS's share loss against UPI is mechanical (UPI started small), so its drawdown and peak timing carry the information.
    • The 12-month average smooths seasonality, so D reacts to a fall with a lag of up to a year.

    8 · Share decomposition

    Own growth against comparison-group growth

    See the decomposition →

    aThe question

    When a rail's share fell, was that because the rail shrank, or because the group (mostly UPI) grew faster?

    bData used

    Volumes of the rail and of every group member. "From" is the group's reference window ( for UPI + IMPS, UPI + cards and cards only; for P2M + cards); "to" is the latest 12 months, .

    cFormulas

    \bar N_0, \bar N_1 = \text{mean own volume in the "from" and "to" windows}; \quad \bar G_0, \bar G_1 = \text{mean group total volume}
    \text{share}_w = \bar N_w / \bar G_w, \qquad \ln\frac{\text{share}_1}{\text{share}_0} = \ln\frac{\bar N_1}{\bar N_0} - \ln\frac{\bar G_1}{\bar G_0}

    The identity is exact: the log change in share is own log growth minus group log growth. "Own growth" and "group growth" in the table are 100 × (N̄1/N̄0 − 1) and 100 × (Ḡ1/Ḡ0 − 1).

    dDecision rule (main driver)

    If the share fell: own decline if the rail's own average volume fell (N̄1 < N̄0), otherwise group grew faster. If the share rose: gained share.

    eWorked example (live numbers)

    fCaveats

    • Here the share is a ratio of window averages, while the classification uses the average of monthly shares, so the two "from" shares differ slightly ( for IMPS).
    • Group growth is dominated by UPI, much of it payments that were never digital, so "group grew faster" does not mean the rail lost customers to UPI.

    9 · Hypotheses

    H1–H3: the Assignment 2 rule and the sharper v2 test

    See H1–H3 →

    cShared tools: the regression design and the drift test

    Two estimators are used throughout. The A2 OLS trend is the regression stated in Assignment 2; the drift is the mean 12-month log change of section 6, applied to the whole window (bases from the window start, and for any series involving total UPI the comparisons straddling Aug 2018 are dropped).

    y_t = \alpha + \beta\, t + \sum_{c=2}^{12}\gamma_c\,\mathbb 1[\text{month}_t = c] + \delta\,\text{Diwali}_t + \sum_{m \in \text{COVID}}\phi_m\,\mathbb 1[t = m] + \sum_j \lambda_j\,\mathbb 1[t \ge d_j] + \varepsilon_t

    t is in years from , so β is the change in y per year (for a log series, 100(eβ − 1)% a year). Diwali months: . One dummy per COVID month: (each such month is fitted exactly, so it has no influence on β). A2 level dummies dj, by the rail the series belongs to (for a ratio, the rail in the numerator): UPI's own series, Aug 2018 (same-account exclusion); card series, (card definition change, PSI splice, bank-wise layout change); IMPS and P2M series, none. Columns that are constant or duplicate in the sample are dropped. Standard errors: Newey–West, Bartlett, lags. One-sided p-values: p(> 0) = 1 − Φ(β̂/SE), p(< 0) = Φ(β̂/SE).

    H1Small payments move to UPI, so IMPS and debit tickets rise

    Data. For IMPS and debit: the log relative ticket rt = ln(ticketown ÷ ticketUPI total) from nominal tickets (*_ticket_rs; the ratio is the same in real terms), the log real ticket (*_real_ticket_rs), and the log nominal tickets. Window – .

    A2 rule (per rail): the A2 OLS trend of rt and the A2 OLS trend of the log real ticket are both positive at 5% (one-sided, p(> 0) < 0.05). "Not rejected" only if this holds for both IMPS and debit; otherwise "Rejected".
    v2 test (per rail), three parts: (1) drift of rt > 0 at 5%; (2) drift of the log real ticket > 0 at 5%; (3) the drift of the rail's own nominal ticket is positive (point estimate), so the relative ticket is not rising only because UPI's ticket falls. Supported if all three hold for both rails; Partially supported if at least one part holds for at least one rail; otherwise Not supported.
    "Of which UPI's own ticket fall" = −drift(ln ticketUPI) ÷ drift(rt): the part of the relative-ticket rise that comes from UPI's falling ticket. For debit, the drift of ln(debit ticket ÷ P2M ticket) is also shown, for information only.

    H2The shifts coincide with COVID, not with zero MDR

    Data. ln volume of IMPS, debit and credit, – ( months); the card regressions are also run without the Nov 2019 dummy, and ln(debit ÷ credit) nets out shocks common to both card types.

    y_t = \text{design}_t + \delta\,\mathbb 1[t \ge \tau] + \theta\,\frac{t-\tau}{12}\,\mathbb 1[t \ge \tau] + \varepsilon_t \qquad \text{(level and slope break at } \tau)

    Search. τ runs over every month after trimming of the sample at each end ( months): – , candidates. A candidate is skipped when the break dummies coincide with the fixed dummies and the model cannot be estimated. τ̂ minimises the sum of squared residuals SSR(τ).

    \text{LR}(\tau) = n\,\frac{\text{SSR}(\tau) - \text{SSR}(\hat\tau)}{\text{SSR}(\hat\tau)}, \qquad \text{95\% confidence set} = \{\tau : \text{LR}(\tau) \le 7.35\}

    7.35 is Hansen's (2000) 95% critical value for inverting the likelihood-ratio statistic of a break date. The page reports the earliest and latest month in the set.

    \text{BIC}_0 = n\ln\frac{\text{SSR}_0}{n} + k_0\ln n,\quad \text{BIC}_1 = n\ln\frac{\text{SSR}(\hat\tau)}{n} + (k_0+3)\ln n,\quad \text{BIC}_2 = n\ln\frac{\text{SSR}(\hat\tau,\hat\tau_2)}{n} + (k_0+5)\ln n

    k0 is the number of regressors without a break. The second break τ̂2 is searched with τ̂ held fixed, at least months from it. The number of breaks (0, 1 or 2) is the one with the smallest BIC.

    Timing label for each series, checked in this order:
    1. No break selected if BIC chooses 0 breaks;
    2. Indeterminate if the confidence set covers both Jan 2020 (zero MDR) and Mar 2020 (COVID);
    3. COVID-timed if τ̂ is in Mar – Dec 2020 and the set does not cover Jan 2020;
    4. Zero-MDR-timed if τ̂ is in Oct 2019 – Feb 2020 and the set does not cover Mar 2020;
    5. otherwise Elsewhere.
    Only IMPS, debit and credit with their full design (the first, second and fourth rows) enter the verdict, and only if BIC selects a break.
    A2 rule: Rejected if more than half of those breaks fall in Oct 2019 – Feb 2020, or none falls in Mar – Apr 2020; otherwise Not rejected.
    v2 test: Indeterminate if at least half (and at least one) are Indeterminate; else Supported if more than half are COVID-timed and the debit ÷ credit trend change at Jan 2020 is not significant; else Not supported if more than half are Zero-MDR-timed; else Partially supported.
    The debit ÷ credit test at the two known dates

    Zero MDR (Jan 2020) applied to UPI and RuPay debit, not to credit cards, so the ratio of debit to credit volume nets out shocks that hit both card types (such as COVID). The model is ln(debit ÷ credit) on a trend, month dummies, a level step and a slope change at τ, a Jun 2021 splice dummy, and one dummy for each month of Mar – Dec 2020 and Apr – Jun 2021, over , with Newey–West (12) errors, fitted once with τ = Jan 2020 and once with τ = Mar 2020. "Significant" means |slope change ÷ SE| > 1.96 at Jan 2020.

    H3Remaining card volume is increasingly credit; P2M's gains slow

    Data. Credit's share of debit + credit volume (share_credit_cc_pct) as log-odds; UPI P2M's share of P2M + debit + credit (share_p2m_pc_pct); debit and credit volumes; credit cards outstanding (credit_cards_lakh).

    \text{logit}(p) = \ln\frac{p}{1-p}; \qquad \text{logit}\Big(\frac{C}{C+D}\Big) = \ln C - \ln D \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta\,\text{logit} = \underbrace{\Delta\ln \bar C}_{\text{credit part}} + \underbrace{(-\Delta\ln \bar D)}_{\text{debit part}}

    For each period, Δ compares the mean volume in the period's first 12 months with its last 12 months. "Debit's part" = 100 × (−Δln D̄) ÷ Δlogit.

    \Delta\,\text{logit}(s^{P2M}_t) = a + b\,\mathbb 1[t \in \text{latest 12 months}] + u_t,\quad t \in \text{Jul 2020–Jun 2021} \cup \text{latest 12 months}

    a is the average monthly gain in P2M's log-odds in its first full year, b how much smaller the gain is in the latest year (Newey–West 12).

    A2 rule: Not rejected if all three legs pass: (1) A2 OLS trend in logit credit share, bridged series, p(> 0) < 0.05; (2) the same on the PSI period only (from ); (3) P2M's average monthly share gain (pp) over the latest 12 months () is smaller than over its first 12 (). Otherwise Rejected.
    v2 test, three parts: (1) slope break: the post-COVID drift of the logit credit share exceeds the pre-COVID drift, (post − pre) ÷ √(SEpre² + SEpost²) > 1.96; (2) debit drove it: debit's part of the post-COVID rise is above 50%; (3) P2M slowed: b ÷ SE(b) < −1.96. Supported if all three hold, Partially supported if one or two, else Not supported.
    Card supply (context, no verdict): between and the latest 12 months, ln growth of credit volume = ln growth of cards in force + ln growth of payments per card; "from new cards" = cards' ln growth ÷ volume's ln growth.

    fCaveats

    • Most log levels look persistent (section 12), so OLS trend t-statistics can overstate significance. That is why the v2 tests use drifts.
    • The H3 A2 leg 3 is mechanically expected: a share near its 100% ceiling must gain fewer points.
    • The break confidence set is reported as its earliest–latest span; it need not be a solid block (debit's set has months inside a -month span).
    • Monthly data cannot separate Jan 2020 from Mar 2020 well: the two dates are only two months apart.

    12 · Diagnostics

    Unit roots, trend tests and residual autocorrelation

    See the diagnostics →

    aThe question

    Do the log series wander like random walks (a unit root) or move around a deterministic trend? The answer decides which trend test to trust.

    bData used

    Every log or logit series used in the tests (volumes, real tickets, relative tickets, the three logit shares and ln debit ÷ credit), over each series' own months in the window. Nominal tickets and real values are left out of the diagnostics table.

    cFormulas

    \text{ADF:}\quad \Delta y_t = a + b\,\tfrac{t}{12} + \rho\,y_{t-1} + c_1\Delta y_{t-1} + c_2\Delta y_{t-2} + \textstyle\sum_{c=2}^{12}\gamma_c\,\mathbb 1[\text{month}_t=c] + u_t, \qquad \text{statistic} = \hat\rho/\text{SE}(\hat\rho)

    Constant, trend, month dummies and 2 lags; ordinary (not HAC) standard error. A unit root is rejected at 5% if the statistic is below (MacKinnon value for a constant and trend, about 100 observations).

    \text{KPSS:}\quad e_t = \text{residual of } y_t \text{ on constant, trend, month dummies};\ S_t = \textstyle\sum_{s\le t} e_s;\ \ \eta = \dfrac{\sum_t S_t^2}{n^2\,\hat\sigma^2},\ \ \hat\sigma^2 = \tfrac1n\textstyle\sum e_t^2 + 2\sum_{l=1}^{12}\big(1-\tfrac{l}{13}\big)\tfrac1n\sum_t e_t e_{t-l}

    Trend stationarity is rejected at 5% if η > .

    \text{Ljung–Box:}\quad Q = n(n+2)\sum_{k=1}^{12}\frac{\hat\rho_k^2}{n-k} \sim \chi^2_{12}

    ρ̂k is the k-th autocorrelation of the regression residuals; a small p-value means the residuals are autocorrelated (another reason for Newey–West errors).

    dDecision rule ("reading")

    trend-stationary if ADF < and KPSS ≤ (both tests agree there is no unit root); unit root likely if ADF ≥ and KPSS > (both point to a unit root); otherwise inconclusive. An asterisk on the results page marks a rejection at 5%.

    The trend-estimates table: four methods

    • Drift: mean 12-month log change, NW(12): as in section 6, on the full window. Valid whether or not the series has a unit root.
    • A2 OLS trend: β in the regression design of section 9.
    • Segmented OLS: the same design plus max(t, 0), a slope change from ; "after Jan 2020" = β + slope change, with its SE from the covariance of the two.
    • A2 OLS trend with cards outstanding and PoS terminals (card volumes only): the A2 design plus ln cards outstanding (own card type) and ln PoS terminals as controls, to separate usage from supply.

    The p column shows p(> 0) / p(< 0); the Ljung–Box p is for the residuals of that regression.

    Glossary

    Terms used on the Final analysis pages

    NPCI
    National Payments Corporation of India, which runs UPI and IMPS and publishes their monthly statistics.
    UPI
    Unified Payments Interface: instant account-to-account payments by phone, using a virtual address or QR code.
    IMPS
    Immediate Payment Service: NPCI's older instant bank transfer, by account number and IFSC or mobile number.
    P2P / P2M
    Person-to-person and person-to-merchant UPI payments. NPCI publishes the split from Apr 2020.
    PSI
    RBI's Payment System Indicators, the monthly release of payment statistics for every system, including card payments (PoS based and Others) and acceptance infrastructure.
    Bank-wise statistics
    RBI's monthly ATM/PoS/card pages, with one row per bank and an all-bank total.
    MDR
    Merchant discount rate: the fee a merchant pays to accept a payment. Set to zero for UPI and RuPay debit cards from 1 Jan 2020; credit cards still carry it.
    Splice, k
    Joining two sources into one series. k is the scale factor (geometric mean of PSI ÷ bank-wise over the overlap) applied to the older source.
    Drawdown
    How far a series is below its own highest level: latest ÷ peak − 1, using 12-month averages.
    Log points
    Differences in natural logs. A change of 0.10 log points is about 10.5%; exactly 100(ex − 1)%.
    Logit / log-odds
    logit(p) = ln(p ÷ (1 − p)). For a share it spreads out values near 0% and 100%, so changes there stay visible.
    HAC / Newey–West
    Standard errors robust to heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation. Bartlett weights 1 − l/13 down-weight lags l = 1 … 12.
    Trimming
    Excluding the first and last 15% of months as possible break dates, so each side of a break has enough data.
    BIC
    Bayesian information criterion: fit (n ln SSR/n) plus a penalty per parameter (ln n); smaller is better.
    ADF, KPSS
    Unit-root tests with opposite null hypotheses: ADF assumes a unit root, KPSS assumes trend stationarity.
    A2 rule / v2 test
    The rejection rule pre-stated in Assignment 2, and the revised, sharper test added after the review.
