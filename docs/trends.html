What the series show

    Card lines are card-network payments only. RBI's daily file counts card payments processed by card networks. RBI's monthly Payment System Indicators also count other card payments. In March 2026 the daily file shows about 56% fewer credit-card payments and 9% fewer debit-card payments than the monthly figures. The credit-card gap has widened since 2023, which fits (but does not prove) RuPay credit cards being used through UPI. Use the daily card lines for the timing of changes (weeks, festivals, weekdays); for total card spending, use the monthly figures on Explore the series.

      Share (i) = system i ÷ (UPI + IMPS + NEFT). This is a share of these three systems only, not of all payments in India (cards, cheques, RTGS and others are left out). Average payment (i) = value (i) ÷ number of payments (i), with value converted from ₹ crore to rupees and volume from million to single payments. An average shows how big a typical payment is; it does not show what the payment was for.

      Analysis E — One supplementary regression

      Analysis E: do more banks on UPI go with more UPI payments?

      Model (OLS, monthly): ln(UPI volumet) = β0 + β1 · ln(banks live on UPIt) + β2 · Trendt + εt. Trendt = 1, 2, 3 … for each month in the sample. Data: NPCI UPI product statistics (volume and banks live). Because both sides are in logs, β1 is an elasticity.

      Analysis E — UPI volume against banks live on UPI (both on log scales)

      Each dot is one month. On log scales, the slope of the cloud is roughly the elasticity before the trend is taken out.

      Analysis E — Actual vs fitted UPI volume

      Fitted = eβ0 + β1·ln(banks) + β2·Trend from each sample. Gaps between the lines are the residuals εt.

        This is an association, not proof that adding banks causes more UPI payments. Bank participation, demand, merchant QR acceptance, smartphone use and policy (demonetisation, COVID, zero MDR) all grew together, so β1 picks up part of them too. Both series rise steadily, so the residuals are strongly autocorrelated (Durbin–Watson well below 2): we report Newey–West standard errors, and the estimates should be read as descriptive.
